Tandem links an Android phone and a Linux computer over Bluetooth and your Wi-Fi: clipboard, notifications, calls, files, and one pair of headphones for both. Free software. No account, no cloud.

Install on both Read the source

Pairs over Bluetooth. The pairing you already did is the trust. No codes, no accounts.

Works with no Wi-Fi. Small things go over Bluetooth; big files and audio wait for a shared network.

Any Linux desktop. GNOME, KDE, sway, Hyprland, X11. One small Python service.

Everything is a switch. Turn on only what you want, from the phone or the terminal.

What crosses the seam

phone → computer computer → phone both ways

  • ↔

    Clipboard

    Copy on one, paste on the other. Text and images. Password-manager copies stay where they are.

  • →

    Sign-in codes

    A one-time code arrives by text, and it's already on your computer's clipboard.

  • →

    Notifications

    See them on the computer, reply to messages, use their buttons. Dismiss on either side and it's gone from both.

  • →

    Calls

    A ringing call shows on the computer with Silence and Decline, and your music pauses until you hang up.

  • ↔

    Files and links

    Share → Send to computer on the phone, tandem send or Send to phone in the file manager. Links open in the other device's browser.

  • →

    Screenshots

    Take one on the phone; it lands in your Pictures folder and on the clipboard.

  • ↔

    Find it

    tandem ring rings the phone at full volume, even on silent. The phone can ring the computer back.

  • →

    Battery

    The phone's and your headphones' battery on the computer, with a warning before they run out.

  • ↔

    Do Not Disturb

    Turn it on on one and the other follows.

  • ←

    Type on the phone

    tandem type sends your real keyboard to whatever field is open on the phone.

  • ←

    Screen mirroring

    tandem screen shows and controls the phone in a window, via scrcpy.

  • →

    Lock on leave

    Walk away with your phone and the computer locks itself.

One pair of headphones, both devices, no glitches

Bluetooth headphones that connect to two devices at once tend to stutter and drop when both play. Tandem keeps them on one link and still lets you hear everything.

The device the headphones are on becomes the hub. If it's the phone, the computer's audio travels over your network and the phone mixes it in. If it's the computer, the phone plays through it like a speaker, and calls still ring on the phone.

Switch the hub from the phone's notification, the app, or tandem switch. Needs both on the same network (Wi-Fi, a hotspot, or Tailscale) and PipeWire on the computer.

Install on both

On the phone Android 13+

Download the APK

Or track updates with Obtainium (add github.com/aidenwboudr/tandem). F-Droid and IzzyOnDroid listings are on the way.

On the computer Linux, systemd, BlueZ

git clone https://github.com/aidenwboudr/tandem && tandem/laptop/install.sh

Debian and Ubuntu can use the .deb on the releases page instead. An AUR package is on the way.

  1. Pair the phone with the computer in Bluetooth settings, like any device.
  2. Open Tandem on the phone. Within a few seconds it asks Pair with your computer? Tap Allow.
  3. Turn on the features you want. Each one asks for what it needs when you switch it on.

What it can see, and where it goes

Nowhere but your computer. No servers, no accounts, no analytics. The phone talks to the one computer it's paired with and nothing else.

Locked to that computer. Network traffic is TLS, pinned to the computer's key from Bluetooth pairing. The computer only accepts the paired phone.

Only what you turn on. Notification access, call state and photos are requested by the switch that needs them, and the app says why. Full details.
